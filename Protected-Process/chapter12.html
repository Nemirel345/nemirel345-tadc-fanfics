Chapter 12

Curtain Call

Jax: Well that sure was something.

The portal closes behind the members, all piled up on the floor in front of the stage.

Daisy: Mom would have liked that one.

Jax: Pfft… I’m sure she would.

Daisy: Is she… still…?

Ragatha: I checked up on her this morning. She said she’s fine, but… she wouldn’t open her door more than a crack.

Jax: Well, at least we know she isn’t dead! She hasn’t abstracted yet. Unless…

Ragatha: Jax!

Jax: What? She’ll come out when she’s done hiding.

Daisy: Mom’s not hiding.

Jax: She is. Just not from us.

Ragatha’s eye flicks to Daisy, then back to Jax.

Ragatha: That’s not what she’s doing.

Jax: That’s exactly what it is.

Ragatha: Don’t make it sound like she’s doing it to be difficult.

Jax: I didn’t say that.

Ragatha: You implied it.

Jax: I implied she’s not coming out.

Ragatha: Because she’s hurting.

Jax: Yeah. And? Doesn’t change the fact she’s not coming out.

Daisy shifts. Her ears flatten.

Ragatha: We can’t just… I don’t want to leave her like that.

Jax: Then what are you gonna do?

Ragatha: I’m going to check in on her again.

Jax: No.

Ragatha: Excuse me?

Jax: She said she’s fine. Through the door. That means Go away.

Ragatha: It really doesn’t. It just means… I’m overwhelmed.

Jax: Same thing.

Ragatha: Jax, she’s—

Jax: Having a moment. Yeah. We all are.

Daisy looks between them.

Ragatha: I’m bringing her tea.

Jax: You’re bringing her guilt.

Ragatha: That’s not fair!

Jax: It’s accurate.

Ragatha’s eye twitches.

Ragatha: You don’t get to decide what helps people.

Jax: And you do?

Ragatha: At least I’m trying!

Daisy takes a step back.

Ragatha sighs.

Ragatha: Fine. Then what do we do?

Jax: Literally anything else.

Ragatha: That’s your plan? Pretend it’s not happening?

Jax: My plan is to stop making it worse.

Ragatha: And what about her?

Ragatha motions to Daisy.

Jax: What about Dee?

Ragatha: She misses her mom.

Jax: Yeah. I know.

Ragatha: So maybe—

Jax: So maybe we don’t stand here like she’s not listening.

Ragatha: I didn’t mean to—

Jax: You never do, Rags. You never mean it.

Ragatha: That’s not… Ugh!

Daisy turns away and leaves.

Ragatha: Daisy! Wait. I didn’t…

Jax: Good job, Rags.

Ragatha: Me? You’re the one that started it!

Daisy slowly walks into the common area.

It’s a lot quieter.

Zooble is already on the couch. Gangle sits beside them, sketching.

Daisy slumps down next to Zooble.

Zooble: What’s… um—

Zooble looks at Daisy, then stops themself.

Daisy sighs.

Daisy: Ragatha and Dad are arguing. Again.

Zooble: Of course they are.

Daisy leans her head back against the couch and exhales.

Zooble: They’re both bad at this. In different ways.

Daisy turns to Zooble.

Zooble: Jax can’t stand not feeling in control. When he’s not able to, he just bails. Because he’s scared. Sometimes it feels like he’s an asshole to people to push them away, for some stupid reason. But I’ll admit, and I hate saying this, he is trying. He has been. Ever since you were born.

Daisy: And Ragatha?

Zooble: Ragatha hovers because she panics. She wants to help, but she’s a little… extra, sometimes. So she bombards you with love and comfort and tea, until you can’t breathe.

Gangle looks up from her sketchbook and nods gently.

Zooble: They’re both trying. Just… neither of them is listening. Not to your mom. Not to you.

Daisy exhales.

Zooble: You didn’t do anything wrong. Leaving was the right call. You don’t owe anyone your patience. Especially when they’re making things worse.

Daisy smiles softly.

Gangle’s pencil moves again, steadily.

Daisy watches for a moment.

Daisy: What are you drawing?

Gangle freezes.

Gangle: Oh, it’s… nothing important.

Zooble: You don’t have to show it. But you can.

Gangle hesitates, then slowly tilts her sketchbook towards Daisy.

Daisy leans in. Her ears perk up.

Daisy: Oh, wow! That’s… really good.

Gangle’s shoulders loosen.

Gangle: T-Thanks.

Daisy: I used to draw when I was little. But that’s way better than my stuff.

Gangle: We could… draw together. Sometime.

Daisy: Yeah. I’d like that.

Daisy keeps leaning over Zooble’s shoulder to peek at Gangle’s sketchbook.

Zooble: Hold on a sec.

Zooble shifts. They stand and drop into the seat on the other side of Gangle.

Zooble: There.

Daisy immediately scoots closer to Gangle.

Daisy: You’re really good at this.

Gangle: Y-you think so?

Daisy: Yeah!

Gangle: I… I don’t usually show my art to anyone. E-except you, Zoobie.

Zooble: I know.

Daisy: But why? It’s really good. Honest.

Gangle: Thanks. I guess… I’m just not really confident in it.

Daisy: You should be. Look at this.

Gangle: Oh, that? That’s nothing. Just a crude sketch.

Daisy: Crude sketch, my a⟦ %$!# ⟧s! That shading is amazing.

Zooble jolts up.

Zooble: Daisy! Language.

Daisy: Ha! That’s rich coming from you.

Zooble: Oh god. You really are his daughter.

Daisy: Is that… bad? Did I say something wrong?

Zooble: No Dee. You’re fine.

Daisy: Oh. I guess I am.

Daisy sticks her tongue out to Zooble.

Zooble: Hey! Not fair. You’re lucky I don’t have one to throw back at you.

Gangle giggles softly.

Zooble: Gangle’s got one. But she refuses to show it to anyone.

Daisy: Is that true?

Gangle: I mean… yeah. Zooble’s seen it a few times.

Zooble: It’s a cute little ribbon sticking out of her mask.

Daisy: No way. I want to see that.

Gangle: I’d… prefer not to.

Daisy: Bummer.

Daisy looks over at the drawing.

Daisy: Those hands you drew. They look weird.

Gangle: Oh?

Daisy: I mean… different.

Gangle: Right. You’ve never seen realistic hands before.

Daisy: Is that what people look like, outside the circus?

Gangle: Yeah. Kinda.

Daisy: Huh.

Gangle: It used to be easier to draw them, back when I had a normal body. I could hold the pencil normally.

Gangle tries to position her pencil on her ribbon, like fingers. She goes back to wrapping the ribbon around it.

Gangle: I miss it sometimes.

Daisy: Can you teach me?

Gangle: To draw hands?

Daisy: Yeah.

Gangle: …Okay. Sometime.

Daisy: Deal.

Gangle: Just… don’t expect miracles.

Daisy: Hey, look how much I’ve learned in the few months I’ve been alive. Besides, Caine fixed my aging thing. I’m human…ish, now. I’ve got time.

Gangle laughs softly. Daisy laughs too.

Jax rounds the corner to the common area.

He notices Daisy sitting with Toybox and Ribbons.

She’s… smiling. Laughing. Happy.

His eyes flick from her to Zooble, then back. His pupils narrow.

He rolls his eyes, looks away, and leaves without a word.

Zooble watches him go, then turns back to Daisy.

Caine: GOOD MORNING, my delightful digital denizens!

Caine hovers above the stage, arms wide.

Caine: Gather ‘round cause today’s adventure is going to be a real —

Caine’s eyes flick between the members in front of him.

Caine: Huh. Looks like we’re missing someone again.

Everyone turns to Ragatha.

She hesitates, looks down, then shakes her head slightly.

Caine: Well! That makes, what? Three adventures in a row?

Bubble: Four if you count the short one!

Caine: It’s… unfortunate. It’s hard to keep running group adventures when people keep opting out…

His gaze drifts to Zooble.

Caine: I suppose I could just… bring her out.

Zooble: What? No! Don’t do that.

Caine: Don’t be dramatic. I’d just give her a little nudge. A friendly reminder that adventure awaits.

Daisy stiffens.

Caine: As the ringmaster, it’s my duty to keep everyone engaged. Can’t have cast members consistently missing out on the — Oh! Hi, Pomni.

All heads turn.

Pomni stands at the edge of the stage. She looks tired.

Daisy: Mom!

Daisy runs. She collides with Pomni, arms wrapping tight. Pomni leans down, arms coming around her.

Caine: — adventures!

Caine spins once mid-air.

Caine: Gather ‘round cause today’s adventure is going to be a real —

Gangle: W-wait. Didn’t you already say that?

Caine: — knee-slapper.

He launches into the rest of the briefing, detailing hazards, objectives, and an alarming amount of glitter.

Daisy doesn’t let go.

Daisy: Mom…?

Pomni looks down at Daisy.

Daisy: Are you going to disappear again?

Pomni’s arms tighten around her. Her voice is quiet.

Pomni: I won’t.
